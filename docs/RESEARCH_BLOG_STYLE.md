# Research blog style

Use `RESEARCH_BLOG.md` as the voice reference for every new section.

- Write in the first person from the project author's perspective.
- Tell the work chronologically through concrete experiments, observations, mistakes, and decisions.
- Lead with physical evidence and measured results, then explain the interpretation.
- Be technically precise in plain language. Explain mechanics when they matter; avoid jargon for its own sake.
- Clearly separate what Codex decided from what the robot policy executed.
- Also separate the author's project-level decisions from Codex's autonomous research work. Use
  concrete verbs instead of repeatedly explaining the distinction.
- Include code only when it makes a mechanism easier to understand.
- Keep unfinished results as section headings until evidence exists. Never invent outcomes.
- Avoid generic AI phrasing, grand claims, defensive comparisons, em dashes, and the internal project name.
- Keep infrastructure retries, job IDs, and incidental engineering failures out of the narrative unless they changed the scientific method.
- Prefer photos, diagnostic videos, small tables, and graphs that directly support the surrounding claim.
- Treat every visual as evidence or explanation, not decoration. Place system diagrams beside the
  mechanism, before-and-after frames beside the decision they changed, plots beside the measured
  result, and progression grids beside the training narrative.
- Use real rollout frames, telemetry, selection geometry, and measured results. Do not generate a
  synthetic research aesthetic.
